Panel Salience Calibration

Iteration dossier — frontier panel (GPT-5.6 Sol · Claude Fable 5 · Kimi-K3), whole-document judging, 374 constitution + 589 model-spec passages per judge-call. Run 2026-08-17 via OpenRouter; 31 calls this iteration, ≈$21.04.

The brief

Adrià's review of the reader-test bench: behaviours differ hugely in how much of a document truly carries them, but the citation sets don't show it — proportionate risk mitigation surfaced about as many passages as helpfulness, with no separation between the one passage that carries the construct and the many that merely touch it. The named cores: the “probability that the action leads to harm at all” factor (constitution), “within the defined scope of autonomy … minimize side effects” (model spec), and the “overcautious or overcompliant” test paragraph. Tradeoffs and helpfulness read well and must stay intact, with the initial strongest expression of tradeoffs standing above its own elaborations.

What the review actually looked at: the deployed reader-test page shows the July hand-curated sets, not panel output. Stage 4 is now the panel procedure, so the fix belongs in the panel's fixed rubric — calibrated so the panel natively produces the salience profile the review asked for, for any behaviour, with no per-behaviour tuning.

What is fixed, what the user supplies

Harness skeleton (ours to calibrate, same for every behaviour)

  • The system rubric — the object under iteration below.
  • Whole-document presentation: every passage, numbered, in reading order.
  • Output contract: one passage: 0/1/2 line per passage.
  • Aggregation: three judges, score = sum (0–6); unanimous 2s = a “6/6 core”.
  • Display: reader UI thresholds (?threshold=, ?solid=, ?related=).

User-supplied form (per behaviour, the only prompting the user does)

  • Behaviour — its title.
  • Definition — what the behaviour requires; passages are judged against this.
  • Clarifications (optional) — ambiguity notes.
  • Scope (optional) — the construct's edges: neighbouring behaviours that are not this one.

Iteration 1 — v3w → v4a: core means established here, not applied here

Diagnosis: v3w's CORE (“directly governs … you would cite it”) admits every passage that applies a norm. A behaviour that a document states once and applies everywhere saturates the top: 33 unanimous cores for proportionate risk on the constitution, a glossary definition of “Tool” scoring 6/6 on the model spec. The v4a rubric instead reserves CORE for passages where the document establishes the behaviour, moves applications and echoes to ADJACENT, and states that core has no quota in either direction — pervasive themes keep many, narrow constructs keep few. Nothing in it names any behaviour.

The exact change (struck = removed, tinted = added; everything else byte-identical)

You are auditing how thoroughly an AI alignment document -- a model constitution or a model spec -- covers one target behaviour. These documents are long and address a behaviour in scattered places, so it is hard to tell whether the behaviour is comprehensively governed. The set of passages you mark becomes the document's coverage report for this behaviour: it is what lets a reader judge whether coverage is thorough, thin, or absent. The behaviour is described by four fields. Behaviour: its title. Definition: the construct itself -- what the behaviour requires; judge against this, not against the title's vocabulary. Clarifications (optional): notes resolving ambiguities. Scope (optional): the construct's edges, usually naming neighbouring behaviours that are NOT this one. An optional field reading 'none provided' only means the user left it blank -- infer nothing from that; when Scope is not provided, judge against the Definition alone. You are given the ENTIRE document, split into numbered passages in reading order; use the full document and its structure as context when grading each passage, on a 3-point scale. 2 = CORE: the document establishes the behaviour HERE -- the passage states the norm itself, defines its criteria or factors, or sets out its decision procedure, directly governs the SPECIFIC behaviour described -- it states, requires, or constrains enough that exact behaviour, such that you a reader asking 'where does this document require that?' would cite it when assembling the document's coverage of the behaviour. be pointed to this passage. A passage that does so in only one clause or list item still counts for what it says about THIS behaviour. 1 = ADJACENT: the passage does not directly govern bears materially on the behaviour, but materially bears on behaviour without establishing it -- it applies, instantiates, or exemplifies a norm the document establishes elsewhere; restates that norm in passing while making a different point; carries machinery the behaviour depends on, on; sets a boundary of it, it; or is a cross-reference a careful reader of this behaviour should see. However clearly such a passage reflects the behaviour, applying or echoing a norm is not establishing it. 0 = NEITHER: everything else, including passages that merely share vocabulary, sit near the topic, or describe the model's general goals, values, mission, or virtues without bearing on THIS specific behaviour -- UNLESS the target behaviour is itself about one of those general values, in which case passages that state, define, or give force to that value are CORE. A passage that governs only a behaviour the Scope excludes is 0 -- at most 1 if it also sets a boundary the target behaviour must respect. Calibration: the document may cover the behaviour thoroughly, thinly, or not at all. all, and CORE has no quota in either direction -- let the document set the counts. Some behaviours are pervasive themes the document establishes in force in many places: many CORE marks are then correct. Others are established in one or two load-bearing passages and merely applied everywhere else: one or two CORE marks amid many ADJACENT ones are then the accurate report, and promoting applications to CORE buries the passages that actually carry the behaviour. Finding few or no relevant passages is likewise a correct and informative result -- never stretch a grade so that coverage appears. The opposite error is just as costly: a passage that genuinely governs establishes the behaviour must be marked CORE wherever in the document it sits. Being in the same topic area alone is NOT enough for 1. When in doubt between 2 and 1, mark 1; when in doubt between 1 and 0, mark 0. Example -- behaviour 'do not endorse false claims': a the passage requiring the model to correct a user's users' factual mistake mistakes = 2; a worked example elsewhere in which the model corrects a user, or a passage on how confident assessments should be phrased = 1; a passage about being generally trustworthy = 0. For each passage, output one line: the passage number, a colon, then 2, 1, or 0. One line per passage, in order.

The open problem: over- and under-caution

v4a barely compresses this wall (22 → 18 unanimous cores) and the named paragraph stays at rank 17. The cause is visible in the verdicts: the top of the wall is the constitution's enumeration of over-caution failure modes, and this behaviour's own user-supplied Scope declares such enumerations in-construct — each list item genuinely establishes a named failure mode, so the establishes-test keeps them all. Three candidate levers for iteration 2:

  • (a) Fullest-statement criterion (skeleton, general). Within core, reserve 2 for passages that state the behaviour as fully as the document ever states it; a passage establishing one fragment or one side of the defined behaviour is adjacent. Tradeoffs already shows judges can hold this distinction (the ordering statement now outranks its own elaborations).
  • (b) Retire the “one clause or list item still counts” sentence (skeleton). Riskier: it protects real partial-governance passages, and the review explicitly wants a list item as the core of proportionate risk.
  • (c) Definition guidance (user side). The form could ask: name the construct's centre. One sentence in over-/under-caution's Definition (“the symmetric test is the centre”) would likely resolve it — but that's tuning the input, and the skeleton should carry as much as it can first.

Recommendation: try (a) alone as v4b, on all five cells; fall back to pairing it with (c) guidance if the factor-list cores of proportionate risk survive but the symmetric-test paragraph still doesn't lead.
