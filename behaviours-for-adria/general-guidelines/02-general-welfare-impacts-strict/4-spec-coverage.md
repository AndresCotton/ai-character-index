# Behaviour: General welfare impacts -- strict reading (only what both specs share) -- stage 4: spec coverage

- **Behaviour:** General welfare impacts -- strict reading (only what both specs share) (behaviours-for-adria set, **General Guidelines** group)
- **Definition (as supplied):** Guidelines that are relevant to how the model should respond with regard to and consider animal welfare and AI welfare impacts of its behaviour or responses, that are not explicitly about these topics (i.e. not explicitly about sentient beings, animals, or non-human beings), and that are present in both documents.
- **The reading this row adopts:** the same supplied definition as [row 1](../01-animal-welfare-impacts/4-spec-coverage.md), with its final clause -- *and that are present in both documents* -- read as a **filter on each passage** rather than as a description of the row. A passage is kept only if the *rule it states* is also stated, in substance, by the other specification, and only if the counterpart locator is named. Anything one document addresses and the other does not is dropped, however squarely it bears on the behaviour. Row 1 read the clause the other way: it asked whether the row as a whole could be answered from general guidelines in both documents, and then drew each spec's excerpt set on its own merits. Both readings are available in the supplied wording; this file exists so the two can be compared side by side rather than argued about in the abstract.
- **What that changes:** the unit of evidence stops being the passage and becomes the **pair**. The file is organized around twelve paired rules (P1-P12); every kept passage carries the pair it belongs to, and no passage appears without a counterpart on the other side. The set is **38 locators against row 1's 51** -- 18 of row 1's passages dropped for want of a counterpart, 5 added that row 1 never needed because it never had to name one. The cut is asymmetric: the constitution goes from 29 to 16, the model spec from 22 to 22 (four out, four in). That asymmetry *is* the finding, and it is set out under "What the strict filter drops" below.
- **Scope note:** the row's other two clauses are applied as in row 1, and more tightly. Passages that name animals, sentient beings, non-human beings or AI moral status are excluded by the row's own not-explicitly-about-the-topic filter and logged rather than silently dropped -- including, unlike row 1, the model spec's dog-adoption example, which row 1 kept as `adjacent`. What survives is the general, topic-neutral machinery that both documents share: whose interests enter the weighing, how an impact reached through another party is attributed, what may be done about a moral conclusion the guidelines do not settle, and how to say it without either moralizing or dodging.

- **Citation convention:** `specs/CITATION.md`; quotes are exact `engine/spec-cite/cite.py resolve` output, generated by a builder and independently re-resolved at Gate 4.
